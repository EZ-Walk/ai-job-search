## 1. How does your lifestyle align with Backroads' values and culture?

I live aboard a 1966 sailboat in Emeryville so nature stays in my days, not just on my weekends. Weather, water, and the outdoors are the conditions I work in, not a hobby I visit.

I also spend a lot of time bringing landlubbers onto the water. Most of them are not sailors. A good day for them is rarely about how well I sail. It is whether I noticed they were cold, explained what was about to happen before it did, and made the experience feel possible instead of intimidating. That is the small version of guest service: a comforting word, a concern anticipated, the day going at their pace.

In consulting I practice the same thing as service-oriented leadership. I work with small-business owners who know their operations better than I ever will. My job is to put the facts and the data in front of us so we can decide together, not to arrive with an answer they have to swallow. Honesty about what the numbers show, and respect for the person who has to live with the decision, is the whole job.

That is the culture I want to work inside: an active life, people treated well, and decisions made with clear information.

## 2. Tell us about your interest in this role and what excites you about the opportunity with Backroads

Backroads gets people outside, moving, and on adventures with each other. I already live that way. What excites me about this role is the work that makes those trips actually exist: which itineraries run, when they depart, and how much capacity they get.

Demand Planner sits between guest demand and real operations. A departure that is over- or under-built is not just a spreadsheet miss. It is someone's week outside. I want to help more people get that week, and I want to do it with the same data-driven operations habit I already use: find the numbers when they are not handed over, read performance honestly, and recommend add, cut, or hold so the people closest to the work can decide together.

I have not held a demand-planning title. Booking pace and assortment planning are new. Pointing that recommendation work at Backroads' trip assortment, so more guests get the outdoor week they came for, is the part that fires me up.
